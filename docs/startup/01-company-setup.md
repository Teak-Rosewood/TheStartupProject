# 01 · Company setup

> Guidance for founders, not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules (especially Indian tax, data-protection and payment rules) change; items marked **(verify)** need a check with a chartered accountant (CA), company secretary (CS) or lawyer before you act.

Do this in roughly this order. Most steps can be done online; budget 2–4 weeks and a few ₹10,000s plus professional fees.

## Structure
- **Private Limited Company** is the usual choice if you plan to raise investment, grant ESOPs or sell to larger customers. It needs at least two directors and two shareholders (one director must be an Indian resident).
- **LLP** is cheaper to run but awkward for equity investment. **Sole proprietorship** is fastest and carries unlimited personal liability; fine for testing an idea, not for taking on contracts or employees.
- Incorporate through the MCA's SPICe+ form, which can also issue PAN, TAN, GST, EPFO and ESIC registrations in one flow. Use a CA or CS for the first filing; it is cheap insurance.
- Check the company name against the trademark register and domain availability **before** you file.

## Founders
- **Founder agreement / shareholders' agreement:** roles, equity split, **vesting (commonly 4 years with a 1-year cliff)**, what happens if someone leaves, decision rights.
- **IP assignment:** every founder, employee and contractor assigns code and designs to the company in writing. Investors check this in due diligence.
- **ESOP pool:** commonly 10% reserved; adopt a scheme with a lawyer before you promise equity to anyone.

## Tax and registrations
- **DPIIT recognition** (Startup India) can unlock tax and compliance benefits; benefits change often, so read the portal's current terms **(verify)**.
- **GST registration:** required once aggregate turnover crosses the threshold (₹20 lakh for services, ₹10 lakh in special-category states **(verify)**), and earlier in some cases (for example supplying through e-commerce operators). Register voluntarily if your customers are businesses that want to claim input tax credit.
- **Exporting software or services to foreign customers:** this is usually a zero-rated supply if you file a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** each year and receive payment in convertible foreign exchange through a bank. Ask your CA about FIRC/e-FIRA documentation and the purpose codes your payment gateway needs **(verify)**.
- Keep a compliance calendar: monthly/quarterly GST returns, TDS deposits and returns, annual company filings (financial statements and annual return), income tax return, board meetings. Hire a CA/CS on a monthly retainer from day one.

## Money
- Open a **current account** in the company's name; never mix founder and company money.
- Connect accounting software your CA already uses (Zoho Books, Tally, Vyapar). The payments module will export invoices as CSV for them.
- Corporate card or virtual card for SaaS subscriptions, so personal cards never pay for production services.

## Brand and domain
- Buy the domain in the **company's** name and put registrar login behind MFA. Enable registrar lock. Add two people to the account.
- File a **trademark** application for the name and logo (software is class 9, SaaS class 42, plus class 35 or 38 if relevant). It is cheap and slow; file early.
- Set up company email (Google Workspace, Zoho Mail). Create role addresses: `support@`, `security@`, `privacy@`, `legal@`, `billing@`, `grievance@`.

## Insurance and contracts
- Consider cyber liability and professional indemnity insurance once you have enterprise customers; some require it.
- Keep templates for customer agreement, NDA, contractor agreement and the [DPA](../legal/dpa.md) in one folder.

## Do not forget
Display the legal name, CIN, registered address and Grievance Officer on the website and in the [Terms](../legal/terms-of-service.md); payment gateways and app stores check for them.
